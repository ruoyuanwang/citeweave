# Digital Twin Research in Healthcare and Technology: A Comprehensive Bibliometric Analysis

## Structured Abstract
**Background:** Digital twin (DT) technology has rapidly emerged as a transformative paradigm across healthcare, engineering, and computer science. This study provides a comprehensive bibliometric mapping of the DT research landscape from 2012 to 2023.
**Methods:** A corpus of 796 unique, deduplicated publications was analyzed using performance analysis, science mapping, and network analysis. Methods included Bradford’s law of scattering, keyword temporal dynamics, and multiple network constructions (co-authorship, institution collaboration, keyword co-occurrence, bibliographic coupling, co-citation, and direct citation) using association-strength normalization and Louvain community detection.
**Results:** The field exhibits explosive growth, with a compound annual growth rate of 70.54% and a peak of 355 publications in 2023. The citation distribution is highly skewed (mean 53.20, median 16.00), with 15.83% of works remaining uncited. IEEE Access leads as the most productive source (24 publications), while Kyle B. Enfield is the most prolific author (22 publications). Conceptual structure reveals a dominant computer science core intertwined with medicine, artificial intelligence, and data science. Social structure analysis identifies distinct collaborative clusters centered around clinical research groups and engineering networks. Intellectual structure, mapped through co-citation and bibliographic coupling, shows a field organized around survey-driven knowledge consolidation and emerging healthcare applications.
**Conclusions:** The DT research domain is characterized by rapid expansion, strong technological foundations, and increasing clinical translation. The field’s intellectual base is concentrated in a small core of highly cited surveys, while its research front is diversifying into personalized medicine, IoT-enabled healthcare, and ethical dimensions.
**Limitations:** Findings are bounded by the source database, retrieval date, and metadata availability. Citation windows are unequal, and network analyses depend on parameterization choices.

## 1. Introduction
Digital twin technology represents a paradigm shift in how complex systems are monitored, simulated, and optimized. Originally conceptualized in manufacturing and engineering, DTs have rapidly permeated healthcare, smart cities, and personalized medicine [E028–E051]. The core premise—a dynamic, bi-directional link between a physical entity and its virtual replica—has generated substantial academic interest, as evidenced by the corpus of 796 unique works analyzed here [E001]. This study employs a rigorous bibliometric workflow, guided by established methodological frameworks [E069–E072], to map the performance, social, conceptual, and intellectual structures of this emerging field. By integrating descriptive metrics with network science, we aim to provide a holistic understanding of the research landscape, its key contributors, thematic evolution, and knowledge foundations.

## 2. Methods
### 2.1 Data Acquisition and Corpus Description
The analysis corpus was constructed through a systematic retrieval process. The source reported 796 results, and all 796 unique source records were successfully acquired, confirming complete retrieval without truncation [E002]. After deterministic exact deduplication, the final canonical corpus comprised 796 unique works [E001]. The temporal span of the corpus extends from 2012 to 2023 [E004].

### 2.2 Data Quality and Preprocessing
Metadata completeness was assessed across key fields. Critical fields such as title, publication date, document type, source identifier, cited-by count, and reference count achieved 100% coverage [E003]. The abstract field was present for 86.18% of works, while publisher information was available for 81.91% [E003]. Digital object identifiers (DOIs) were present for 98.24% of the corpus, and language metadata covered 94.6% of works [E003]. All works were classified as journal articles, with no review-type documents identified in the source metadata [E014].

### 2.3 Analytical Procedures
Performance analysis included whole counting of publications by year, source, author, institution, and document type. Citation impact was assessed using source-reported citation counts, with descriptive statistics including mean, median, percentiles, and zero-citation rates [E015–E017]. Bradford’s law of scattering was applied to identify the core journal zone [E019].

Science mapping employed multiple network constructions. Co-authorship networks were generated using full counting with a minimum occurrence threshold of 2, yielding 59 displayed nodes and 128 edges [E024]. Institutional collaboration networks used a minimum occurrence of 2, displaying 72 nodes and 153 edges [E026]. Keyword co-occurrence analysis applied a minimum occurrence threshold of 5, resulting in 72 displayed nodes and 188 edges [E027]. Bibliographic coupling was constructed using a sparse shared-reference self-join with a minimum of 5 references per document and 2 shared references, displaying 72 nodes and 230 edges [E022]. Co-citation analysis used a minimum occurrence of 4, displaying 72 nodes and 203 edges [E025]. Direct citation networks, with a minimum occurrence of 15, yielded a sparse structure of 5 nodes and 4 edges [E023]. All networks employed association-strength edge normalization and Louvain community detection [E072], with layout optimization using VOS-style or ForceAtlas2 algorithms [E071].

Keyword temporal dynamics were analyzed by tracking unique works per normalized keyword and publication year for the globally most frequent terms [E020]. A three-field plot visualized relationships among leading authors, sources, and keywords [E021].

## 3. Results
### 3.1 Performance Structure: Growth, Productivity, and Impact
The DT research domain demonstrates extraordinary growth dynamics. From a nascent stage with only 1 publication in 2012, the field remained quiescent until 2018 (4 publications), after which it entered a phase of exponential expansion [E007]. The annual output surged from 18 publications in 2019 to 355 in 2023, representing a compound annual growth rate of 70.54% over the observed non-zero endpoints [E006, E007]. The year-over-year increase peaked at 120 publications between 2021 and 2022, with 2023 adding 118 new works [E007]. This trajectory, visualized in Figure 1 [E052], indicates that the field is in a rapid growth phase with no signs of saturation.

Source productivity analysis reveals a highly scattered publication landscape. IEEE Access ranks as the most prolific venue with 24 publications, followed by the International Journal of Advanced Research and Innovations (19) and Zenodo (16) [E008, E009]. The Bradford distribution confirms this dispersion: Zone 1 (the core) comprises only 37 sources contributing 262 documents, while Zone 2 requires 169 sources and Zone 3 encompasses 262 sources to cover the remaining thirds of the literature [E019]. This pattern, illustrated in Figure 8 [E059], indicates that DT research is published across a broad range of outlets without a dominant core of specialized journals.

At the author level, Kyle B. Enfield emerges as the most productive researcher with 22 publications, substantially ahead of Paramesh Shamanna (11) and Zhihan Lv (10) [E010, E011]. Institutional productivity is led by Johns Hopkins University (12 publications), followed closely by Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique, Uppsala University (11 each), and several institutions with 9 publications including Harvard University, Imperial College London, Maastricht University, Stanford University, and University of Oxford [E012, E013].

The citation impact distribution is highly skewed. The mean citation count of 53.20 far exceeds the median of 16.00, indicating that a small number of highly cited works drive the average upward [E015, E017]. The 90th percentile reaches 109 citations, while the 95th percentile stands at 182.25 [E017]. The maximum citation count is 2,623 [E017]. Notably, 126 works (15.83%) have received zero citations, though this figure is influenced by the recency of many publications [E016]. The most-cited document, “Digital Twin: Enabling Technologies, Challenges and Open Research” (2020), has accumulated 2,623 citations, followed by “Industry 5.0: A survey on enabling technologies and potential applications” (2021) with 1,798 citations [E018]. The top-cited works are predominantly survey and review articles published between 2019 and 2022, reflecting their role as foundational knowledge syntheses [E018].

### 3.2 Social Structure: Collaboration Networks
The co-authorship network reveals a fragmented collaborative landscape organized into 10 distinct clusters [E024, E061]. The most prominent cluster centers around a clinical research group including Paramesh Shamanna (weighted degree 77.0), Terrence Poon (67.0), Mohamed Thajudeen (65.0), Mala Dharmalingam (58.0), and Ashok Keshavamurthy (57.0) [E024]. This tightly connected component suggests an established clinical research team with sustained collaborative output. Other clusters appear more loosely connected, indicating that while individual research groups are productive, cross-group collaboration remains limited.

The institutional collaboration network, comprising 72 nodes and 153 edges across 10 clusters, shows a more interconnected structure [E026, E062]. Maastricht University (weighted degree 36.0), Stanford University (35.0), and the Electrophysiology and Heart Modeling Institute (35.0) emerge as central nodes, bridging multiple institutional clusters [E026]. The presence of European institutions (Inserm, Imperial College London, KU Leuven, Sorbonne Université) alongside North American and Asian universities indicates a globalized research enterprise with significant transatlantic and transpacific linkages [E013, E026].

### 3.3 Conceptual Structure: Thematic Landscape and Evolution
Keyword co-occurrence analysis maps the conceptual terrain into 8 thematic clusters [E027, E063]. The dominant node is “Computer science” (occurrences: 607, weighted degree: 4,577), which anchors a cluster encompassing “Artificial intelligence” (252 occurrences), “Data science” (205), and “Engineering” (172) [E027]. A second major cluster revolves around “Medicine” (350 occurrences), connected to “Health care” (190), “Internal medicine” (106), and “Political science” (86) [E027]. The proximity of these clusters in the network space (within-between distance ratio: 0.278) indicates substantial interdisciplinary integration between computational and clinical domains [E027].

Temporal analysis of keyword dynamics reveals the field’s conceptual evolution [E020, E060]. “Computer science” has been present since the earliest publications in 2012 and has grown to 256 documents in 2023 [E020]. “Medicine” shows a similar trajectory, rising from 1 document in 2012 to 155 in 2023 [E020]. “Artificial intelligence” emerged in 2014 and accelerated sharply after 2019, reaching 108 documents in 2023 [E020]. “Health care” demonstrates particularly rapid recent growth, more than doubling from 48 documents in 2022 to 101 in 2023 [E020]. The keyword overlay visualization (Figure 16) confirms that recent research (mean publication year 2022.55) concentrates on healthcare applications, while earlier work (mean year 2020.95) focused on foundational computer science and engineering concepts [E067]. The density map (Figure 17) identifies “Computer science,” “Medicine,” and “Artificial intelligence” as high-density areas of research activity [E068].

The three-field plot reveals specific author-source-keyword relationships [E021]. Mohsen Guizani connects “Computer science” with IEEE Internet of Things Journal, while Kyle B. Enfield bridges multiple conceptual domains—including “Digital health,” “Data science,” “Political science,” and “Health care”—across venues such as Journal of Personalized Medicine, IEEE Internet of Things Journal, and Multimedia Tools and Applications [E021]. This pattern illustrates how prolific authors serve as knowledge integrators across disciplinary boundaries.

### 3.4 Intellectual Structure: Knowledge Base and Research Fronts
The co-citation network, representing the intellectual base of the field, comprises 72 nodes and 203 edges organized into 7 clusters [E025, E064]. The most co-cited references (identified by OpenAlex IDs) form a dense core with high weighted degrees: W2941876953 (1,359.0), W2910597635 (1,178.0), and W2988359971 (1,160.0) [E025]. The high within-between distance ratio (0.867) indicates that these foundational references are highly integrated across clusters, suggesting a shared knowledge base that transcends thematic boundaries [E025].

The bibliographic coupling network, representing the research front, displays 72 nodes and 230 edges across 8 clusters [E022, E066]. The most central documents include “Impactful Digital Twin in the Healthcare Revolution” (weighted degree 911.0), “Digital Twin Perspective of Fourth Industrial and Healthcare Revolution” (735.0), and “Developments of digital twin technologies in industrial, smart city and healthcare sectors: a survey” (698.0) [E022]. These works share extensive reference lists, indicating that the current research front is organized around comprehensive surveys that synthesize knowledge across application domains. The within-between distance ratio of 0.397 suggests moderate cluster differentiation, with distinct research fronts emerging in healthcare applications, industrial implementations, and technological infrastructure [E022].

The direct citation network is notably sparse, with only 5 nodes and 4 edges forming a single cluster [E023, E065]. This sparsity, despite a minimum occurrence threshold of 15, indicates that direct citation linkages within the corpus are limited. The field appears to draw heavily on external foundational literature rather than forming dense internal citation networks, consistent with a rapidly expanding domain where new entrants frequently cite seminal external works.

## 4. Discussion
The integrated findings reveal a research field in a state of dynamic emergence. The explosive growth trajectory (70.54% CAGR) and the concentration of output in the most recent years (355 of 796 publications in 2023 alone) indicate that DT research has transitioned from a niche interest to a mainstream academic pursuit [E006, E007]. This growth is not merely quantitative; the conceptual structure shows genuine interdisciplinary integration, with computer science and medicine forming overlapping rather than segregated knowledge domains [E027].

The performance structure reveals a field without a dominant journal core. The Bradford distribution, with only 37 journals in Zone 1, indicates that DT research is published across diverse outlets spanning engineering, medicine, and computer science [E019]. This scattering pattern is typical of emerging interdisciplinary fields where no single disciplinary journal has established dominance. The high mean-to-median citation ratio (53.20 vs. 16.00) and the concentration of citations among survey articles suggest that the field’s impact is driven by knowledge synthesis rather than primary empirical contributions [E015, E018].

The social structure analysis reveals a tension between local cohesion and global fragmentation. While individual research groups, such as the clinical team centered on Shamanna and Poon, exhibit strong internal collaboration, the overall co-authorship network remains fragmented into 10 clusters [E024]. Institutional collaboration shows greater integration, with European and North American institutions forming interconnected hubs [E026]. This pattern suggests that while interpersonal research collaborations are still forming, institutional-level partnerships are more established.

The conceptual evolution from technological foundations to healthcare applications is clearly documented in the keyword temporal dynamics. The rapid recent growth of “Health care” (101 documents in 2023) and the emergence of “Human–computer interaction” and “Computer security” as significant themes indicate a maturing field that is addressing implementation challenges and user-centered design [E020]. The intellectual structure, as revealed by co-citation analysis, shows a highly integrated knowledge base, while bibliographic coupling reveals differentiated research fronts organized around application domains [E022, E025].

The ethical dimension, represented by works such as “Digital Twins in Health Care: Ethical Implications of an Emerging Engineering Paradigm” (653 citations), indicates that the field is engaging with the societal implications of its technological trajectory [E018, E037]. The presence of “Political science” as a significant keyword (86 occurrences) further suggests that governance, policy, and regulatory considerations are becoming integral to the research discourse [E020].

## 5. Limitations
Several limitations constrain the interpretation of these findings. First, the corpus is bounded by the source database, query parameters, and retrieval date; publications outside these boundaries are excluded [E002]. Second, citation counts are source- and retrieval-date-dependent, and works published in later years have had less time to accumulate citations, introducing a temporal bias in impact assessment [E015–E017]. Third, network analyses depend on parameterization choices, including occurrence thresholds, normalization methods, and community detection algorithms; different parameterizations could yield different structural representations [E022–E027]. Fourth, metadata coverage varies across fields; abstract availability at 86.18% and publisher information at 81.91% may affect certain analyses [E003]. Fifth, document-type classification inherits source practices, and the exclusive classification of all works as articles may not capture the full diversity of publication types [E014]. Finally, keyword frequencies depend on source metadata quality and synonym normalization, and the incomplete final year (2023) may affect trend interpretations [E020].

## 6. Conclusion
This bibliometric analysis reveals digital twin research as a rapidly expanding, interdisciplinary domain characterized by explosive growth, strong technological foundations, and accelerating clinical translation. The field’s performance structure is marked by high concentration of impact in a small core of survey articles, scattered publication venues, and a skewed citation distribution. The social structure shows fragmented co-authorship networks alongside more integrated institutional collaborations. The conceptual structure demonstrates genuine integration of computer science and medicine, with a clear temporal evolution from technological enablers to healthcare applications. The intellectual structure rests on a highly integrated knowledge base of foundational references, while research fronts differentiate into application-specific clusters. As the field matures, future research is likely to deepen the clinical translation of digital twin technology while addressing emerging challenges in ethics, security, and human-computer interaction.
