# Mapping the Microplastics Research Landscape: A Bibliometric Analysis of Performance, Social, Conceptual, and Intellectual Structures (2004–2019)

**Structured Abstract**

**Purpose:** This study provides a comprehensive bibliometric overview of the microplastics research domain, analyzing its performance, social, conceptual, and intellectual structures from its emergence through 2019.

**Design/methodology/approach:** A corpus of 3,973 unique journal articles was analyzed [E001]. Performance was assessed through publication and citation metrics. Social structure was mapped via co-authorship and institutional collaboration networks. Conceptual structure was explored using keyword co-occurrence networks and temporal trends. Intellectual structure was examined through document co-citation and bibliographic coupling networks, all employing association-strength normalization and Louvain community detection [E022–E027].

**Findings:** The field exhibits explosive growth, with a compound annual growth rate of 29.35% and a peak of 1,329 publications in 2019 [E005, E006]. The research is concentrated in a core of environmental science journals, led by *Marine Pollution Bulletin* [E008]. The social structure reveals a highly collaborative but fragmented community, with distinct Chinese and European research clusters [E024, E026]. Conceptually, the domain is anchored by the central theme of "Microplastics," with a clear temporal evolution from marine environmental science toward chemistry, toxicology, and human health concerns [E020, E027, E067]. The intellectual base is built on seminal works on marine microplastic contamination, while the research front is organized around environmental distribution, freshwater/terrestrial systems, and ecotoxicological impacts [E022, E025].

**Originality/value:** This analysis integrates multiple bibliometric methods to provide a holistic, evidence-based map of the microplastics research field's rapid maturation, highlighting its shifting thematic frontiers and collaborative dynamics.

## 1. Introduction

The proliferation of plastic pollution has emerged as one of the most pressing environmental challenges of the 21st century. Within this broad concern, the study of microplastics—plastic particles smaller than 5 mm—has rapidly evolved from a niche area of marine science into a major interdisciplinary research field. This report provides a systematic, evidence-based bibliometric analysis of the microplastics research domain, aiming to map its performance, social, conceptual, and intellectual structures. By integrating multiple analytical dimensions, we move beyond a simple listing of metrics to offer a synthesized interpretation of how the field has grown, who its key contributors are, what thematic areas define it, and which foundational works underpin its development.

## 2. Methods

The analysis is based on a frozen evidence bundle derived from a deterministic, reproducible workflow. The corpus was acquired from a single bibliographic source, with cursor pagination confirming the acquisition of 3,995 unique source records from an expected 4,002 results [E002]. After exact deduplication, the final canonical corpus comprises 3,973 unique works, all classified as journal articles [E001, E014]. The analysis period spans from 2004 to 2019 [E004].

Metadata completeness was assessed for key fields, revealing full coverage for critical variables like publication year, citation counts, and document type, but lower coverage for abstracts (43.32%) [E003]. Performance analysis involved descriptive statistics for annual output, source, author, and institution productivity, and citation impact. Social structure was examined through co-authorship and institutional collaboration networks. Conceptual structure was mapped using keyword co-occurrence networks and temporal trends. Intellectual structure was analyzed via document co-citation and bibliographic coupling networks. All networks were constructed using association-strength normalization and Louvain community detection, with layouts optimized for clarity [E022–E027, E072]. The methodological framework is informed by established guidelines for bibliometric analysis and science mapping [E069–E071].

## 3. Results and Discussion

### 3.1. Performance Structure: Growth and Impact

The microplastics research field is characterized by a period of slow, foundational growth followed by explosive expansion. From a modest output of 28 publications in 2004, the field experienced a compound annual growth rate of 29.35% over the observed period, culminating in a peak of 1,329 publications in 2019 [E005, E006, E007]. This trajectory, visualized in Figure 1 [E052], shows a clear inflection point around 2015, after which annual output more than doubled every two years. This pattern suggests the field has transitioned from an emergent phase to a period of rapid mainstream consolidation.

The citation distribution reflects this rapid growth and the presence of several highly influential, early foundational works. The mean citation count is 201.34, but the distribution is highly skewed, with a median of 61.00 [E015]. The 90th percentile is 553 citations, and the maximum is 7,932 [E017]. A substantial portion of the corpus (22.78%, or 905 works) has received zero citations, a figure that is partly attributable to the large volume of very recent publications that have had minimal time to accumulate citations [E016]. The most-cited documents are predominantly early reviews and empirical studies that defined the scope of the marine microplastics problem, such as "Microplastics in the marine environment" (2011, 7,932 citations) and "Microplastics as contaminants in the marine environment: A review" (2011, 5,961 citations) [E018]. These works form the canonical knowledge base of the field.

The research output is concentrated in a core of environmental science and pollution journals. *Marine Pollution Bulletin* is the most prolific venue with 395 publications, followed by *Environmental Pollution* (323) and *The Science of The Total Environment* (243) [E008, E009]. A Bradford analysis reveals an extreme concentration of sources: a nucleus of just 4 journals accounts for over a third of all publications, while a long tail of 894 sources comprises the final third [E019]. This indicates a well-defined core literature for the field. The most prolific author is Dario Mendez-Cuadro with 127 publications, and the most productive institution is the Chinese Academy of Sciences with 109 publications [E010, E012]. The presence of "Dialnet (Universidad de la Rioja)" and "Physics of the Solid State" among the top sources, and authors like B. K. Kardashev, suggests the corpus may contain a minor but distinct cluster of non-environmental research, possibly related to materials science, which is a limitation to be considered when interpreting thematic purity [E009, E011].

### 3.2. Social Structure: Collaboration and Community

The social structure of microplastics research is both highly collaborative and fragmented into distinct national and institutional clusters. The co-authorship network, comprising 59 prominent nodes and 121 edges, reveals 10 distinct research communities [E024]. The largest clusters are centered around prolific researchers like Huahong Shi (East China Normal University) and Tamara S. Galloway (University of Exeter), who act as hubs within their respective collaborative groups [E024]. The network's high modularity, with a within-between distance ratio of 0.0, indicates that while intra-cluster collaboration is strong, inter-cluster links between these major research groups are relatively sparse [E061].

This pattern is mirrored and geographically anchored in the institutional collaboration network. The network of 72 leading institutions forms 10 communities, with the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique (CNRS) as the most central nodes [E026]. The network structure shows a clear separation between a large European-centered cluster, which includes CNRS, Ifremer, and the University of Exeter, and a distinct Chinese cluster, comprising the Chinese Academy of Sciences, East China Normal University, and the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences [E026, E062]. This geographic and institutional bifurcation suggests that while the global research effort is immense, knowledge integration across these two major poles may be a challenge and an opportunity for the field's future development.

### 3.3. Conceptual Structure: Themes and Their Evolution

The conceptual core of the field is unequivocally defined by the term "Microplastics," which is the most frequent keyword with 2,770 occurrences and the most central node in the co-occurrence network [E020, E027]. The keyword co-occurrence network, with 72 nodes and 202 edges, is organized into 7 thematic clusters [E027]. The largest cluster is a dense, central group of overarching environmental terms including "Environmental science," "Biology," and "Ecology," which are tightly interwoven with "Microplastics" [E027]. Surrounding this core are more specialized clusters representing distinct research sub-domains, such as a cluster focused on "Chemistry" and "Environmental chemistry," and another on "Pollution" and "Fishery" [E027].

The temporal dynamics of these keywords reveal a clear conceptual evolution. The keyword trend analysis shows that while "Microplastics" and "Environmental science" have grown in parallel, terms like "Chemistry" and "Biology" have seen particularly sharp increases in the later years, especially from 2017 onward [E020]. This shift is visually confirmed by the keyword temporal overlay network, where the average publication year of keywords ranges from approximately 2011.95 to 2017.89 [E067]. Earlier research is associated with foundational environmental and ecological terms, whereas more recent research fronts are colored by keywords related to chemistry, toxicology, and human health, such as "Chemistry," "Environmental chemistry," and "Pollution" [E067]. This indicates a maturation of the field from initial discovery and environmental monitoring toward mechanistic and impact-focused research.

The three-field plot further integrates these conceptual and social structures, showing how leading authors bridge specific sources and themes. For instance, Dario Mendez-Cuadro and Huahong Shi are strongly associated with "Microplastics" and "Environmental science" in *Environmental Pollution* and *Marine Pollution Bulletin*, while Albert A. Koelmans links "Chemistry" and "Environmental chemistry" with *Environmental Science & Technology* [E021]. This illustrates the micro-specialization of research groups within the broader conceptual landscape.

### 3.4. Intellectual Structure: Knowledge Base and Research Fronts

The intellectual structure of the field, representing its knowledge base, is revealed through the document co-citation network. This network of 72 highly cited references forms 4 distinct clusters, representing the foundational pillars of the field [E025]. The largest and most central cluster is anchored by seminal works on the global distribution and quantification of microplastics in the marine environment, such as the reviews by Andrady (2011) and Cole et al. (2011) [E025]. These works, identified by their OpenAlex IDs, form the common intellectual ground cited across the entire domain.

The research front, representing the active areas of current investigation, is mapped by the bibliographic coupling network. This network of 72 recent, highly referenced documents also partitions into 4 clusters, which are thematically distinct from the co-citation clusters [E022]. The most central documents in this network are recent reviews that synthesize the state of the art and set future agendas. Key works include "Microplastics in the environment: A critical review of current understanding and identification of future research needs," "Environmental occurrences, fate, and impacts of microplastics," and "Microplastics in freshwater and terrestrial environments: Evaluating the current understanding to identify the knowledge gaps and future research priorities" [E022]. The clusters in this network point to the major contemporary research frontiers: one focused on the broad environmental distribution and fate, another on freshwater and terrestrial ecosystem impacts, and a third on the ecotoxicological effects and mechanisms of harm. The direct citation network, though sparser, confirms the central role of key empirical studies like "Accumulations of microplastic on shorelines worldwide: sources and sinks" in directly influencing subsequent work [E023].

## 4. Limitations

The findings of this report are bounded by several limitations inherent to the data and methods. The corpus is derived from a single bibliographic source at a specific retrieval date, meaning citation counts are time-dependent and may not reflect the most current impact [E015, E017]. The final year of analysis, 2019, may be incomplete if the retrieval occurred before year-end [E005]. Metadata coverage is imperfect; notably, only 43.32% of works include an abstract, which limits the depth of text-based analysis [E003]. The presence of seemingly off-topic sources and authors, such as *Physics of the Solid State*, suggests the retrieval strategy may have introduced a small amount of noise, and findings should be interpreted with this in mind [E009, E011]. Network analyses are descriptive and parameter-dependent; the displayed nodes and edges are a filtered subset of the full candidate network, and centrality measures do not imply causal or substantive importance [E022–E027]. Finally, all productivity and citation metrics are descriptive and must not be interpreted as measures of research quality.

## 5. Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis maps the rapid and accelerating evolution of microplastics research from a nascent field into a major interdisciplinary scientific endeavor. The performance structure shows explosive growth concentrated in a core of environmental journals, driven by a set of highly cited foundational works. The social structure is characterized by strong intra-national collaboration, particularly within distinct Chinese and European clusters, with opportunities for greater global integration. Conceptually, the field has matured from a primary focus on marine environmental presence to a more complex landscape encompassing chemistry, toxicology, and human health. The intellectual base is firmly rooted in seminal marine pollution studies, while the research front is actively pushing into freshwater, terrestrial, and biological impact domains. This integrated evidence map provides a comprehensive baseline for understanding the development and current state of microplastics research.
